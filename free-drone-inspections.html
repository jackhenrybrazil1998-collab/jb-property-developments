What this service involves

What a drone inspection can show

Depending on visibility and access, aerial images may help identify slipped or broken tiles, ridge concerns, damaged chimney details, visible flashing defects, moss build-up and blocked gutters.

What happens during the inspection?

We discuss the roofing concern, assess whether drone use is suitable and safe, and look at the relevant roof areas. We then explain visible findings and whether a closer physical inspection is advisable.

What a drone cannot confirm

An aerial view cannot always reveal problems hidden beneath tiles, felt, insulation or roof structure. Some defects need a closer inspection or investigation to establish their cause.

Using an inspection to plan repairs

If an issue is visible, we can discuss practical repair options and provide a free quotation where work is needed. An inspection is not an obligation to book further work.

What an aerial roof inspection may reveal

A free drone roof inspection can help homeowners understand visible roof condition without immediately arranging extensive access. The photographs can provide useful context for discussing a repair, though they are not a guarantee that every issue has been found.

  • Missing, cracked or slipped tiles and slates on pitched roofing.
  • Ridge and verge defects visible from suitable viewing angles.
  • Obvious chimney pointing deterioration or displaced roof flashings.
  • Debris, vegetation or visible overflow areas at gutters and valleys.
  • Surface damage and exposed edges on some flat-roof coverings.
  • Potential concerns around rooflights, extension junctions and parapets.
  • Areas that may warrant a closer hands-on inspection before a quotation.

How free drone inspections work

Tell us the property location, the roof concern and whether there is access to a safe operating area. We consider weather, lawful flight restrictions, privacy, nearby obstacles and people.
