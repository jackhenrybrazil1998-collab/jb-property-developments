What this service involves

When a flat roof needs attention

Warning signs can include recurring damp patches, blistering or splits in the covering, standing water, loose edge details and leaking roof outlets. The first step is to identify whether the problem is local or affects the roof as a whole.

Three-layer felt roofing

We work with traditional three-layer felt systems where suitable for the roof and project. Good preparation, correct laps, weatherproof junctions and finishing details are essential to lasting performance.

Flat roof repairs versus replacement

A small damaged area may be repairable if the surrounding material and deck remain sound. If the covering is generally worn or water has affected underlying materials, replacement may be more appropriate. We explain options following an assessment.

Edges, gutters and lead details

A flat roof can leak at its abutments, upstands, flashings, outlets and perimeter trims as well as through the main covering. We look at these details when assessing the whole problem.

Flat roof repairs, three-layer felt and replacement options

A flat roof should have enough fall to guide water towards its outlets, with sound waterproofing at edges, upstands and joins to walls. Leaks may come from a split felt seam, an outlet problem, trapped water or failed detailing around a skylight. Locating the weak point can help distinguish a localised repair from a more extensive replacement.

When is a new felt roof worth considering?

Where an existing covering is brittle, blistered or repeatedly leaking, a replacement may be more sensible than another patch. We discuss traditional three-layer felt roofing, edge details and the suitability of the underlying structure before quoting. Associated gutter and rainwater drainage problems should be addressed as part of the overall repair plan.

Do flat-roof leaks always mean the whole roof must be re-felted?

No. Some isolated defects are repairable, but the covering's age and general condition matter. For other defects, browse roof repairs or ask about a suitable free drone roof inspection to identify visible issues.

What a felt-roof assessment should cover

A watertight flat roof relies on more than the visible felt surface. Edge trims, outlets, rooflight details and the way the covering meets brickwork all matter. The underlying deck also needs to be sound before a new system is installed. Persistent water retention can point to an outlet or fall problem that should not simply be hidden with another layer.

  • Check felt seams for separation, cracks, splits and blistering.
  • Inspect upstands at adjoining walls and around rooflights.
  • Assess outlets and rainwater flow, especially where pooling recurs.
  • Check edge trims, drip details and joints with gutters.
  • Review the roof deck for movement or evidence of moisture damage.
  • Consider insulation and ventilation implications when renewing a covering.
  • Discuss the suitability of traditional three-layer felt for the roof design.
  • Identify access, weather windows and waste-handling requirements.

Common flat-roofing questions

Can you patch a felt roof? Some isolated defects can be repaired with suitable materials, but extensive wear, poor substrate condition or repeated failures may justify replacement.

Is water standing on my roof always a leak? Not automatically. Persistent standing water deserves checking because it can expose vulnerable details and may indicate poor drainage.

Can a felt roof go over an extension? Flat-roof work on suitable extensions is common, but the proposed covering must match the existing structure, falls, upstands and relevant requirements.
