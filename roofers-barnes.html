Roof problems to look out for around Barnes

Barnes homes include older pitched roofs, chimney stacks, parapet details and extensions with separate flat roof coverings. A wet patch upstairs may be caused by a displaced slate or tile, but water can also track in around flashings, valleys or a party-wall junction. Identifying the route of the leak matters more than simply covering the visible stain.

Barnes roofing checklist for pitched roofs and older properties

Roof details can be especially important on homes with chimneys, parapets, valleys or previous extensions. A leak near a party wall does not necessarily mean the adjoining roof covering has failed. Likewise, missing mortar around a ridge and displaced slates call for different remedies. Consider these points when discussing an inspection:

  • Check slate and tile condition around chimney stacks and roof junctions.
  • Ask whether lead flashings are secure and correctly dressed at abutments.
  • Look for slipped ridge tiles, cracking to bedding and signs of repeated movement.
  • Investigate gutter overflow, debris from nearby trees and blocked downpipes.
  • Review rainwater disposal where a flat-roofed rear addition meets a pitched roof.
  • Consider fascia or soffit deterioration where persistent overflow wets the eaves.
  • Use a suitable aerial inspection only where flying conditions and rules permit.

Common questions from Barnes property owners

Will gutter cleaning stop damp walls? It can help if overflow is the cause, but defective joints, pointing or other sources of damp may also need attention. A diagnosis should come before any claim that cleaning will solve it.

Can you repair a leak without stripping the whole roof? Sometimes a specific tile, flashing or valley fault can be addressed locally. Broader work is considered if the surrounding materials are no longer dependable.
