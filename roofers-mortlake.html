Start with what the water is doing

Overflowing gutters

Water pouring behind a gutter can leave marks on fascia boards and upper walls. We check blockages, joint condition and downpipe discharge before proposing bigger work.

Chimney junctions

Weathered pointing and damaged lead can allow rainwater to enter close to a stack. A visible damp patch downstairs is not reliable proof of the precise point of entry.

Moss and roof debris

Build-up can affect water flow, particularly into valleys and gutters. We offer manual moss removal and appropriate cleaning while taking care with the existing covering.

Flat-roof edges

A felt roof may be intact across its centre but weak at an upstand or roof edge. These vulnerable details should be examined before recommending a complete new covering.

Tracking roof leaks and blocked rainwater routes in Mortlake

If water shows up around an upper-floor window or ceiling, the roof covering might be responsible—but overflowing gutters, chimney joints or a flat-roof edge can look similar from inside. A useful inspection follows the likely route of rainwater, not just the visible patch.

  • Check open gutters and downpipes for accumulated leaves and roof debris.
  • Inspect brackets, joints and end caps for leaks and movement.
  • Look at slipped slates, damaged tiles and insecure ridges on pitched roofs.
  • Review chimney stacks and lead flashings at roof intersections.
  • Examine valleys for obstructions and deteriorated adjoining coverings.
  • Assess flat rear roofs for cracking, seam defects and blocked outlets.
  • Consider whether repeated leaks justify a wider roof-condition review.

Mortlake roof maintenance FAQs

Can moss or leaves in a gutter cause a wall to look damp? Yes, overflow may wet the brickwork, although masonry defects can also allow water in. Both drainage and wall condition may need checking.

Is repointing a chimney always a roofing solution? No. Mortar defects are one possibility; the leadwork and nearby roof may be the real source of a leak.

What should I describe when calling about a leak? Tell us when the stain develops, whether it is worse in driving rain, and whether the roof is pitched or flat. Photographs from a safe position can help.
