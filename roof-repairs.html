What this service involves

Finding the source of a roof leak

Water can travel along timbers and roof coverings before it appears as a ceiling stain. We check likely entry points such as tile laps, ridge details, chimney junctions, valleys, flashings and flat-roof edges. A visible stain alone does not always identify the fault.

Tile, slate and ridge repairs

Broken or slipped roof tiles and slates can leave the covering exposed. We assess whether a local repair is suitable and check adjoining tiles and fittings rather than replacing sound materials unnecessarily.

Storm and emergency roof repairs

High winds, heavy rain and fallen debris can create urgent roofing problems. Contact our 24-hour advertised emergency service to describe the issue, discuss safety and arrange an assessment. Response timing depends on conditions and availability.

Repair or replace?

A small isolated fault may be repairable. Widespread ageing, repeated leaks or damaged underlying materials may call for a larger repair or replacement. We explain what we find and provide a quotation before agreed work.

Common causes of leaking roofs and urgent repair enquiries

Water stains do not always show where rain entered. A missing or cracked tile, loosened ridge, damaged flashing, blocked valley, rooflight junction or failing felt edge can all lead to leaks. A sensible roof repair starts with finding the likely entry point and checking the surrounding covering before deciding on the scope of work.

Emergency roof repairs, storm damage and slipped tiles

If high winds have moved slates or a leak has started during heavy rain, avoid going onto the roof. Describe the problem when you call our 24/7 emergency roofing contact service. We can discuss safe steps, inspection arrangements and a free quotation, while any call-out remains subject to conditions and availability.

Should I repair a roof or replace it?

A single damaged tile may justify a local repair, while repeated leaks across an extensively worn covering may indicate wider work. For a deteriorating flat roof, see felt and flat roofing; for widespread replacement, see new roofs. Problems around brick stacks may need chimney and leadwork repairs.

Roof repair assessment: what should be checked?

Before a contractor quotes for a leaking roof, it helps to establish whether the failure concerns the main covering, its junctions or the route of rainwater. Replacing one visible broken tile will not solve a defective valley or wall flashing. A sensible recommendation sets out the likely cause and the areas to be repaired.

  • Replace or refix broken and slipped tiles or slates where appropriate.
  • Check ridge and verge details for loose or deteriorated components.
  • Examine valleys and gutters for trapped debris and water overflow.
  • Inspect chimney flashings and adjacent mortar for gaps and movement.
  • Review rooflight, vent and abutment details where leaks are recurrent.
  • Assess flat-roof felt seams, upstands and outlets for cracks or separation.
  • Identify storm-damaged areas and any immediate risk from loose materials.
  • Explain when local repair is sensible versus widespread roof renewal.

Questions about emergency roof repairs and leaks

Is a dark ceiling patch always a fresh leak? Not always. Some marks are historic; an active leak usually needs its timing, moisture and surrounding roof defects assessed.

Can a repair be carried out in heavy rain? Weather and safety can limit access and the materials that can be installed reliably. We can discuss urgent protective measures where suitable without promising unsafe immediate work.

Will a free drone inspection identify the source? It may show obvious displacement or deterioration, but it cannot reveal every hidden defect. Physical inspection may still be necessary.
